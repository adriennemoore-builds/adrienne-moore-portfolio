# Case Study: Building My Own Course of Study
### When there's no institution built for what you actually want to learn, you build it yourself.

**The Problem**

I wanted to understand something most people only argue about: how media manufactures racial narratives, and how white supremacy updates itself without needing new laws to do it. Think-pieces and threads weren't going to get me there. I needed theory, history, political economy, and real cross-racial and global analysis — not vibes, not curated TikTok clips, not someone else's summary.

No existing program was built for exactly this angle — global in scope, cross-racial in analysis, connecting media infrastructure to slow violence to language to the Second Amendment in one throughline. So instead of waiting for someone to build it, I designed it myself.

**The Approach**

I built a 13-module, PhD-seminar-level course of study from scratch:

- Sourced and vetted the actual scholarly material — theory, history, political economy — myself
- Sequenced it into modules that build on each other the way a real doctoral program would, from foundational media theory through to a capstone
- Developed my own original framework along the way, including a module on multiracial complicity in maintaining white supremacy — the idea that media narratives can't take hold without participation from non-Black groups, and that colorism and the model-minority myth function as tools of control
- Connected media theory to a live, personal application: the Second Amendment's racialized history, and what it means for a Black woman to legally arm herself with full knowledge of a system historically built to exclude her from that right
- Built a tracking system (part of the Saunders Suite) to hold myself to reading logs, writing benchmarks, and module completion while doing this alongside a full-time job and a business

**What It Proved**

Nobody assigned this. There's no institution checking whether I finish it. The rigor is self-imposed because I decided the questions mattered enough to answer properly, not because a syllabus told me to. That's the same instinct behind every go-to-market strategy I've built with no existing playbook to follow: find what's actually true, build the structure to hold it, and follow through without waiting for someone else to hand you the framework.
